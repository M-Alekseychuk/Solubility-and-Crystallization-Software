# Core Crystallization Process Engineering

## 1. Crystallization Thermodynamics & Phase Boundaries

Crystallization occurs within the metastable zone—the region bounded by the **equilibrium solubility curve** ($C^*$) and the **critical supersaturation limit** ($C_{crit}$):

```
Concentration (C)
      ▲
      │       LABILE ZONE (Spontaneous Uncontrolled Nucleation)
      │  ──────────────────────────────────────────────── C_crit = S_crit · C*
      │       METASTABLE ZONE (Controlled Growth & Secondary Nucleation Suppression)
      │  ┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄┄ Operating Trajectory
      │  ──────────────────────────────────────────────── C* (Equilibrium Saturation)
      │       UNDERSATURATED ZONE (Dissolution Region)
      └───────────────────────────────────────────────────► Temperature (°C) / Anti-Solvent
```

### 1.1 Supersaturation Metrics

- **Supersaturation Ratio ($S$)**:
  $$S = \frac{C}{C^*(T, x)}$$
- **Relative Supersaturation ($\sigma$)**:
  $$\sigma = \frac{C - C^*}{C^*} = S - 1$$
- **Concentration Driving Force ($\Delta C$)**:
  $$\Delta C = C - C^*(T, x)$$

### 1.2 Operating Regimes
- **$S < 1.0$ (Undersaturated)**: Any added seeds will dissolve. Seeding must be prohibited.
- **$1.0 < S \le S_{target}$ (Optimal Seeding Window)**: Seeds survive and provide surface area for gentle, controlled crystal growth.
- **$S_{target} < S < S_{crit}$ (Metastable Growth)**: Growth dominates over nucleation. Ideal for controlled cooling.
- **$S \ge S_{crit}$ (Labile Boundary Breached)**: High risk of uncontrolled primary homogeneous/heterogeneous nucleation, fine formation, and solvent inclusion.

## 2. Seed Loading & Surface Area Kinetics

Seeding bypasses the uncontrolled primary nucleation barrier, dictating the final crystal size distribution (CSD) and preventing batch-to-batch variation.

### 2.1 Seed Mass Calculation
$$W_{seed} = W_{API} \cdot \left(\frac{\text{Seed Loading \%}}{100}\right)$$

### 2.2 Particle Size & Specific Surface Area ($SSA$)
Assuming spherical or pseudo-isometric crystal geometry with median particle size $D_{50}$ ($\mu\text{m}$) and crystal density $\rho_{c}$ ($\text{g/cm}^3$):

$$SSA = \frac{6}{\rho_c \cdot D_{50}}$$

*Example*: For $\rho_c = 1.25\text{ g/cm}^3$ and $D_{50} = 25\ \mu\text{m}$:
$$SSA = \frac{6}{1.25 \times 10^6\text{ g/m}^3 \times 25 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}} = 0.192\text{ m}^2/\text{g}$$

### 2.3 Total & Volumetric Surface Area in Slurry
- **Total Seed Surface Area ($A_{total}$)**:
  $$A_{total} = W_{seed} \cdot SSA\quad [\text{m}^2]$$
- **Volumetric Surface Area ($A_V$)**:
  $$A_V = \frac{A_{total}}{V_{slurry}}\quad [\text{m}^2/\text{L}]$$

Targeting $A_V \ge 0.5\text{ m}^2/\text{L}$ ensures sufficient crystal surface is present to consume supersaturation generated by cooling without breaching $S_{crit}$.

## 3. Cooling Trajectory Design: Cubic vs. Linear

### 3.1 The Flaw of Linear Cooling
In a constant cooling rate regime ($dT/dt = \text{const}$):
$$T(t) = T_0 - \left(\frac{T_0 - T_f}{\tau}\right) t$$

Because the solubility slope $dC^*/dT$ increases exponentially with temperature, cooling at a constant rate in the early stages when total crystal surface area is low rapidly exceeds the metastable limit, causing severe fines generation.

### 3.2 Controlled Cubic (Mullin-Nývlt) Cooling
To maintain a constant, safe relative supersaturation throughout the batch duration $\tau$:

$$T(t) = T_0 - (T_0 - T_f) \left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^3$$

- **Early Phase ($t \approx 0$)**: Cooling is exceptionally slow ($dT/dt \approx 0$), allowing seeded crystals to establish growth faces without nucleation.
- **Late Phase ($t \approx \tau$)**: As total crystal mass and surface area expand dramatically, the cooling rate accelerates, rapidly depleting solute to maximize product yield.

```
Temperature (°C)
  T_0 ─┐
       │\
       │ \   Linear (Uncontrolled)
       │  \
       │   ╲───
       │       ╲
       │        ╲  Cubic Controlled Trajectory
  T_f ─┴─────────┴────────► Time (hours)
       0                  τ
```

## 4. Anti-Solvent Crystallization

When cooling alone provides insufficient yield or when API is thermally labile, an anti-solvent (poor solvent) is metered into the batch:

- **Equilibrium Solubility Drop**: Described by composition-dependent models (Jouyban-Acree or Yalkowsky):
  $$C^*(w_{anti}) = C^*(0) \exp\left(-\alpha \cdot w_{anti}\right)$$
- **Dosing Schedule**: Controlled volumetric dosing ramps the anti-solvent addition slowly during initial seeding and accelerates towards batch completion.

---

## 5. Filtration, Deliquoring & Cake Washing

To ensure complete solvent recovery and prevent API re-dissolution:
1. **Cake Volume Estimation**:
   $$V_{cake} = \frac{W_{API, recovered}}{\rho_c \cdot (1 - \epsilon_{porosity})}$$
   Typical cake porosity $\epsilon \approx 0.40 - 0.50$.
2. **Wash Solvent Volume**:
   Recommended $1.5 - 2.5\times$ cake volumes of cold pre-saturated or low-solubility wash solvent.
3. **Deliquoring Time**:
   Calculated based on pressure differential ($\Delta P$), cake permeability, and filtrate viscosity.
